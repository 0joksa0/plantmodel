\begin{abstract}
Mechanistic models of plant growth usually prescribe carbon input or compute it
from a photosynthesis routine that is solved separately from stomatal
conductance. This work reports a modular simulation model in which the supply
and demand sides of leaf gas exchange are closed against each other at every
evaluation: a Jarvis conductance and a Farquhar assimilation function are
combined into a quadratic in the intercellular CO$_2$ concentration and solved
in closed form, replacing a fixed-point iteration that we show admits a
period-two orbit under water stress. Around this closure the model integrates
eleven states covering starch, sucrose and vacuolar sugar, nitrogen and
phosphorus acquisition with explicit costs, and leaf and root biomass. Carbon
conservation over the carbohydrate subsystem closes to machine precision.
Rather than fitting coefficients per experimental condition, we hold a single
parameter set across four photoperiods and report what that costs: relative
growth rate is reproduced with a mean absolute percentage error of 8.58~\% and a
worst case of 11.75~\%, against a legacy per-photoperiod baseline whose apparent agreement is
a bisection residual of eight coefficients fitted to eight observations.
Leave-one-photoperiod-out cross-validation gives a held-out error of 13.18~\%
for a photoperiod-independent growth coefficient and 4.36~\% when the
coefficient is allowed a linear photoperiod dependence. Diel starch requires only a
near-unity multiplicative alignment, between $0.86$ and $1.15$, whereas sucrose
requires a factor of two to three, which we report rather than absorb. Ablation shows that computing the carbon input, rather than prescribing
it, is what carries the photoperiod response. Variance-based sensitivity
analysis attributes only 20~\% of output variance to parameters acting alone,
placing the remainder in interactions rather than
main effects. Finally we locate one boundary of the model's diel starch balance: under the
reference chamber forcing the diel starch balance returns to its dawn value up
to a 15~h photoperiod, whereas from 16~h the pool gains carbon on every cycle
and still drifts upward after 90 simulated days. Under the higher light dose of
the shipped configuration the balance does not return at any photoperiod tested,
so the boundary is a property of the forcing as well as of the photoperiod. We
characterise this limitation rather than conceal it.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The simulation code, configuration files, reference data and the scripts that
generate every table and figure in this paper are available at \FILLINrepo. The

verification, convergence and carbon-closure studies are released as the
documents cited in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}. The diel carbohydrate
reference data are redistributed from the supplementary material of
\citet{Sulpice2014} under the terms of that publication.
